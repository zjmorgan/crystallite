crystallite.visualization.orientation module#

Inverse-pole-figure (IPF) orientation colouring: the colour of a grain is set by the crystal direction that lies along a chosen sample direction, reduced into the fundamental sector of the crystal’s symmetry.

Pipeline, per orientation R (crystal axes as columns, in the sample frame, as in crystallite.microstructure, so v_crystal = R^T v_sample):

  1. crystal direction d = R^T s of the sample direction s;

  2. symmetry reduction: of the images S d over the symmetry operations S, keep the one closest to a fixed reference direction (a Dirichlet cell of the group, which is a fundamental sector for any point group);

  3. colour, from the position of the reduced direction in the sector.

The symmetry is a point group (crystallite.material.symmetry.point_group_operations(), standard setting: principal axis along z, secondary axis along x), applied at one of three levels (symmetry=):

"laue" (default)

The point group with the inversion: directions d and -d share a colour, as they do in diffraction.

"holohedry"

The highest point group of the crystal system (m-3m, 6/mmm, …): the conventional EBSD colour keys, which merge directions that the crystal’s own point group tells apart.

"point_group"

The point group itself, so polar crystals keep d and -d apart.

Colour keys. A key must give symmetry-equivalent directions one colour and vary continuously, also across the sector edges, where the directions on either side are equivalent to directions just inside the sector [2].

A group generated by its mirror planes has a sector (a chamber) bounded by mirrors on every side: a spherical triangle (m-3m, -43m, 6/mmm, -6m2, -62m, 4/mmm, mmm), a lune (mm2, 3m, 4mm, 6mm) or a hemisphere (m). Its colour key is white at the centre and fully saturated on the edges, where no condition applies. On a triangle each channel is linear in d, 1 at its vertex and 0 on the opposite edge, weighted so that the centroid is white, then normalized by the largest channel [1]. On a lune or a hemisphere, the hue is the azimuth about the centre and the saturation the fraction of the way to the edge.

Any other group G has sector edges joined by rotations. If it is an index-2 subgroup of a mirror-generated group G', its sector is two chambers of G', a chamber C and its mirror image across a mirror of G' outside G. Each direction is reduced into C by G', recording whether that took an operation outside G. Directions that did not are coloured by the white key of C; directions that did are coloured by its black counterpart (the white key less its grey part: the same hue, black at the centre). The two keys agree on the edges of C, so the colours are continuous everywhere [2]. This covers every point group except -1, -3 and -4. These reverse orientation without mirrors, so identifying equivalent directions turns the sphere into a projective plane, which does not fit one-to-one into the 3D colour space [2]. They are coloured by the key of their proper rotation subgroup (1, 3 and 4) instead: still one colour per orientation, since orientations are only defined up to the proper rotations, but d and -d get different colours. This applies to triclinic crystals at every level, and to point groups 3 and -3 at the "laue" level.

Sectors of polar groups reach into the lower hemisphere. Plots show them as two stereographic panels side by side: the upper hemisphere projected from the -z pole, the lower one from the +z pole.

One of the 32 point groups, e.g. "m-3m", "6/mmm", "-43m".

Level at which the point group’s symmetry is applied (see the module docstring).

Attributes:
groupstr

The point group whose operations reduce the directions: the one set by symmetry, or for -1, -3 and -4 its proper rotation subgroup 1, 3 or 4 (see the module docstring).

operationsndarray of shape (n, 3, 3)

Normal of the mirror that splits the sector into a white and a black half, or None for a group generated by its mirrors (all white).

Inward unit normals of the great circles that bound the sector (none for point group 1, whose sector is the whole sphere).

verticesndarray of shape (m, 3)

Sector corners, the one nearest the crystal z axis first, the rest by increasing azimuth: for a triangle bounded by mirrors, the red, green and blue vertices.

labelslist of str

Direction indices of the vertices (miller_label()).

white_pointndarray of shape (3,)

Centre of the white half (the area centroid for a triangle).

black_pointndarray of shape (3,) or None

Centre of the black half.

hemispherestuple of str

("upper",), or ("upper", "lower") for a sector that reaches into the lower hemisphere.
